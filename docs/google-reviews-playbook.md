# Google reviews: how to actually get them

Reviews are the strongest signal in the local map pack, which is where most
accountant enquiries begin. A practice trading since 1980 with no reviews looks
newer and smaller than one trading since 2019 with thirty.

The target is not a hundred. **Twenty honest reviews puts SCA ahead of every
other accountant on the South Coast**, and the first ten do most of the work.

## Before asking anyone

1. Claim and verify the **Google Business Profile**. Nothing else can start
   until this exists.
2. Fill it in completely: services, hours, the Amanzimtoti address, real photos
   of the office and the team, and a description matching `backlink-plan.md`.
3. Copy the **short review link**: Google Business Profile → Read reviews →
   Get more reviews → copy link. It looks like `https://g.page/r/XXXX/review`.
4. Paste it into `src/lib/site.ts` under `google.reviewUrl`. The site's `/review`
   link then sends people straight to the review box.

## The ask

Timing matters more than wording. Ask when someone has just been helped: a
refund came through, a return was filed early, a SARS problem went away, a
year-end pack landed on time. Not in February when everyone is busy.

**By WhatsApp** (highest response rate, use it first):

> Morning [Name] — glad that's sorted. Small favour: would you mind leaving us a
> Google review? It's the main way other local businesses find us. Takes two
> minutes: sca-za.com/review — thanks, Neil

**By email**, after a piece of work:

> Subject: That's filed — and a small favour
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Your [return / reconciliation / registration] is done and submitted.
>
> If you have two minutes, a Google review would help us more than anything
> else. It is how businesses on the South Coast decide who to phone.
>
> sca-za.com/review
>
> Honest is more useful than glowing — if something could have gone better, say
> so. Thank you either way.
>
> Neil Oberholzer
> South Coast Advisory · 031 903 4787

**In person**, which works best of all: "Would you mind leaving us a Google
review? I'll WhatsApp you the link now." Then actually send it while they are
still sitting there.

## Make it easy to say yes

- Put `sca-za.com/review` in the **email signature** of everyone at the practice.
- Add a line to **invoices**: "Happy with the service? sca-za.com/review"
- Print a **small card for reception** with a QR code pointing at
  `https://www.sca-za.com/review`. Any free QR generator will do.
- Add it to the footer of the **monthly management accounts** email.

## Rules, so this never backfires

- **Never offer anything in exchange for a review.** No discounts, no entry into
  a draw. It breaches Google's policy and it is the fastest way to have reviews
  removed.
- **Never write reviews yourself**, or ask staff or family to. It is obvious, it
  is dishonest, and it puts the whole profile at risk.
- **Never review-gate** — do not ask people how they feel first and only send the
  link to the happy ones. Google prohibits it. Ask everyone.
- Ask **one at a time**. A personal message from Neil gets replies; a bulk mail
  gets ignored.

## Responding to reviews

Reply to every single one, within a few days. Replies are public and are read by
people deciding whether to phone.

- **Good review:** thank them by name, mention the specific work, keep it to two
  sentences. No copy-paste replies — five identical thank-yous look automated.
- **Critical review:** thank them, acknowledge the specific problem, say what has
  changed, offer to talk offline. Never argue, never blame the client, never
  mention confidential details of their affairs. A calm reply to a poor review
  persuades more readers than the review itself dissuades.
- **Suspected fake:** report it through the profile, then reply factually stating
  there is no record of them as a client.

## Keep it going

Reviews decay in influence as they age, so a steady trickle beats a burst.

- Ask **two or three clients a month**, every month, tied to completed work.
- Put a recurring monthly reminder in Neil's diary.
- Track it: how many asked, how many landed. If the conversion is under a third,
  the timing of the ask is wrong, not the wording.

## Reviews vs testimonials

They do different jobs, so collect both.

| | Google review | Website testimonial |
|---|---|---|
| Helps you get **found** | Yes — strongest local ranking signal | No |
| Helps you get **chosen** | Somewhat | Yes — longer, specific, on the page |
| Who controls it | Google | You, with the client's approval |
| Where it is asked for | `/review` | `/share-your-experience` |

When someone sends a website testimonial, reply with thanks and the Google link.
Many will happily do both.
